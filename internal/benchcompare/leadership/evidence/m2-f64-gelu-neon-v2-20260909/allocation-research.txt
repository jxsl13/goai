Conclusion
==========

The frozen evidence correctly rejects V2 under the original, unrelaxed promotion
rule. This research does not rescore it and does not recommend a waiver. It also
does not establish that V2 added an allocation path to Softplus, Sigmoid, or SiLU
backward. The source evidence is against that interpretation: between the pinned
old runtime dd1e779eb085bb621ed5dafff0a4351636b6e656 and V2 runtime
361955ac35eaada696045f2cf828817b650174ec, the control benchmark harness and the
relevant Execute, tensor allocation, CPU scheduler, Softplus, Sigmoid, and SiLU
backward execution paths are byte-for-byte unchanged. The relevant diff is only
the GELU gate split plus the new ARM64 GELU implementation/tests and documentation.

The observed +1..+4 B/op parallel-control medians are compatible with benchmark
accounting noise. Go 1.27.1 computes benchmark memory results from two
process-wide runtime.MemStats snapshots around the timed region, then reports the
integer quotient of total bytes by the adaptively chosen iteration count. Thus a
reported B/op value is neither an operation-local attribution nor an exact total,
and identical allocs/op does not imply identical total allocated bytes. This is a
plausible explanation, not demonstrated causality. Only a predeclared old-old,
equal-N diagnostic with raw totals can distinguish baseline measurement movement
from a candidate-specific effect. No new implementation or benchmark-contract
revision is justified before that diagnostic is independently reviewed and run
under a new task.

Frozen evidence and observed pattern
====================================

The independent performance verifier reports: all 24/24 large public targets and
48/48 small controls pass; no GELU B/op or allocs/op median increases; no fixed
control allocs/op median increases. At GOMAXPROCS=12 only, median B/op increases
appear for campaign 1 Sigmoid (+1), all three Softplus campaigns (+2,+3,+2), and
campaigns 2/3 SiLUBackward (+4,+1). Individual exact rank p-values are 0.0816 to
0.9202. Nonsignificance is not evidence of equality and does not erase the
separate repeated-median veto. Conversely, repeated rounded medians are not
source-level attribution.

The raw stream makes the accounting sensitivity visible. Serial Softplus samples
are consistently 2,097,448 B/op and 5 allocs/op. Parallel examples vary around
2,097,480..2,097,486 B/op and 6 allocs/op while adaptive N varies materially
(examples include N=3,367, 3,957, 4,023, 4,290). Sigmoid has observed N values of
10,000 and approximately 12,000 while Softplus and SiLUBackward have lower,
independently calibrated N values. Therefore each printed unit change represents a
different range of possible total-byte deltas in different processes. The raw
text retains B/op and N, not MemBytes itself: for printed q and N, exact MemBytes
is only known to lie in [q*N, (q+1)*N-1]. Medians of q values do not commute with
division by differing N.

Exact source findings
=====================

1. Benchmark boundary and call path

backend/cpu/bench_test.go:18-28 defines benchOn. It creates the backend/context
before ResetTimer, calls b.ReportAllocs and b.ResetTimer, then each timed iteration
calls backend.Execute. BenchmarkSoftplusF64_256K_cpu (lines 56-58) supplies one
prebuilt 262,144-element input. Execute resolves the already registered kernel,
then softplusKernelCPU calls Contiguous (a no-copy return for this fixture),
tensor.NewOn for the output, and parallel(len(out), closure). The returned output
becomes unreachable after the iteration.

At GOMAXPROCS=12, backend/cpu/cpu.go parallelWork uses the persistent worker pool,
creates a per-call poolBarrier, submits poolTask values carrying the closure and
barrier pointer, runs the first chunk, may drain work, and waits. Runtime scheduling
determines whether tasks are received, stolen/drained, or executed inline. At
GOMAXPROCS=1 it directly invokes body(0,n), explaining why the additional parallel
overhead/accounting opportunity is absent. This scheduler source is identical in
old and V2.

Sigmoid follows the same Execute -> tensor.NewOn -> parallel shape, but allocates
a 65,536-element output. Softplus allocates a 262,144-element output. Their main
per-operation output backing arrays are exactly 524,288 and 2,097,152 bytes,
respectively; the remaining printed bytes cover Tensor/Storage metadata, return
slices, closures/scheduler effects, and any process activity captured by the
global counters. Their kernel source is unchanged.

SiLUBackwardF64 follows Execute -> siluBackwardF64KernelCPU. Per iteration it
allocates both a 262,144-element output and a 262,144-element sigmoid scratch
(`make([]float64, len(ds))`), then calls parallel once. This explains its roughly
4,194,7xx B/op and one more allocation than forward controls, but that path too is
unchanged between pins. The GELU V2 files do not call these control functions.

2. Go 1.27.1 benchmark accounting

The pinned SDK source is
/private/tmp/goai-go1271-j1cLvq/go/src/testing/benchmark.go. B.StartTimer reads
runtime.MemStats and records Mallocs and TotalAlloc (lines 138-147). B.StopTimer
reads it again and accumulates differences into b.netAllocs and b.netBytes (lines
151-162). runN invokes runtime.GC, resets counters, starts the timer, runs the
benchmark, and stops it (lines 197-220). launch predicts and changes N until the
requested duration is met (lines 327-360). BenchmarkResult stores aggregate
MemAllocs/MemBytes, while AllocsPerOp and AllocedBytesPerOp return integer division
by N (lines 541-597); fractional bytes/allocations are truncated in output.

The pinned runtime source is
/private/tmp/goai-go1271-j1cLvq/go/src/runtime/mstats.go. MemStats.TotalAlloc is
documented as cumulative bytes allocated for heap objects and never decreases;
Mallocs is the cumulative heap-object count (lines 55-89). ReadMemStats stops the
world and returns allocator statistics current at the call (lines 350-364).
readmemstats_m populates TotalAlloc from the runtime-wide totalAlloc and Mallocs
from nMalloc (around lines 500-512). There is no benchmark-goroutine or operation
filter. Allocations by any goroutine in this test process between snapshots can
enter the delta. The benchmark framework serializes benchmark runN calls with
benchmarkLock, but that does not suspend runtime/background goroutines or worker
pool activity; worker allocations are intentionally part of the operation, while
unrelated process allocations are not distinguishable in this metric.

3. Old versus V2 deterministic source delta

`git diff dd1e779e 361955ac -- backend/cpu/bench_test.go backend/cpu/cpu.go
backend/cpu/elementwise.go backend/cpu/activation_bwd_f64.go backend/execute.go
tensor/tensor.go tensor/storage.go` changes only eight comment/gate lines in
elementwise.go and activation_bwd_f64.go: GELU uses the new vgeluF64Fast gate
instead of vexpF64Fast. cpu.go, bench_test.go, Execute, Tensor/Storage, and the
control function bodies have no diff. ARM64 adds vgelu_f64_arm64.go and initializes
GELU SIMD constants at process startup, outside benchOn's ResetTimer. No static
source path connects those additions to a timed control call.

This rules out an explicit control-source allocation addition. It does not prove
machine-code identity or exclude indirect binary-wide effects: added code/data can
change layout, initialization history, GC/scheduler state, or timing, which changes
adaptive N and opportunities for process activity. No disassembly/object comparison
for all control call paths was retained, so exact emitted-code identity remains
unknown. Even identical emitted control functions would not make the process-wide
counter operation-local.

Interpretation and uncertainty
==============================

Most likely hypotheses to test, without ranking them as established causes:

* Integer quotient plus unequal adaptive N: small non-per-iteration byte totals
  are divided by N; changing N changes q even when the operation allocation path
  is the same. Repeated Softplus q=2,097,484 may be a stable quotient regime rather
  than evidence of a new operation-local allocation.
* Process-wide/runtime and pool effects: worker scheduling, goroutine/runtime
  bookkeeping, and GC work can contribute heap allocations within snapshots. The
  parallel-only occurrence supports investigating this, but is not proof.
* A candidate-wide emitted-code or escape-analysis difference despite unchanged
  control source. This is less directly supported but cannot be excluded without
  binary-level and raw-total diagnostics.

The evidence does not support claiming: (a) the V2 GELU shortcut caused these
bytes; (b) nonsignificant p-values prove no effect; (c) unchanged allocs/op proves
unchanged allocation behavior; or (d) a chosen tolerance would make the original
qualification pass. External host activity affects timing and adaptive N, but OS
processes do not directly allocate on the Go test process heap; only effects
mediated through this process/runtime are relevant to TotalAlloc.

Phase A — mandatory old-old negative control
---------------------------------------------

Use two immutable copies (or two verified paths) of the exact old binary, labeled
old-A and old-B. This detects label/order/accounting movement when runtime code is
identical. Use at least three alternating, count-seven paired campaigns at
GOMAXPROCS=1 and 12 for exactly the three controls. Alternate A/B arm order by pair
and process-count order by campaign as in the frozen protocol. Do not compare an
old run from V2 evidence with a new diagnostic run; both arms must be contemporaneous.

Phase B — equal fixed-N old/V2 diagnostic
-----------------------------------------

Before Phase A, choose and freeze an exact N that is safe for every selected
benchmark/process cell; 1024 is a defensible predeclared value below all observed
completed N values in the frozen stream. Use the same N for A and B and later for
old and V2. Invoke the matched diagnostic binaries with the anchored diagnostic
Test regex, `-test.benchtime=1024x`, and `-test.count=1`, one heavy process at a
time. Fixed-N, not equal duration, is the essential control. Repeat with the same
predeclared paired design. Phase B may proceed regardless of Phase A's outcome,
but interpretations must include Phase A.

Raw totals
----------

Standard benchmark text is insufficient because it exposes only truncated B/op
and allocs/op. The lowest-perturbation option is a new diagnostic-only `_test.go`
file in package cpu_test whose Test function calls `testing.Benchmark` with each
existing benchmark function (`BenchmarkSoftplusF64_256K_cpu`,
`BenchmarkSigmoidF64_64K_cpu`, and `BenchmarkSiLUBackwardF64_256K_cpu`). After
each call returns, print the public BenchmarkResult fields N, MemBytes, and
MemAllocs, plus `MemBytes/N`, `MemBytes%N`, `MemAllocs/N`, and `MemAllocs%N`.
`testing.Benchmark` constructs a normal testing.B and runs the existing benchmark
function through its normal runN/StartTimer/StopTimer machinery; therefore this
preserves benchOn, Execute, kernel, timer, and allocation accounting instead of
duplicating timer brackets or attempting to access internal start/end fields.

Build matched diagnostic binaries from old and V2 with exactly the same added
diagnostic file and compiler settings, verify their hashes, and retain the
original frozen binaries untouched. Invoke the diagnostic Test with an exact
fixed count, for example the predeclared `-test.benchtime=1024x` supplied to both
matched binaries; testing.Benchmark consumes the registered benchTime setting.
Run one labeled binary/process-count arm per process and print only after each
Benchmark call returns so diagnostic formatting is outside the measured region.
Use the same matched diagnostic binaries for old-A/old-B and old/V2 phases. Any
outside-loop ReadMemStats, GC count, or pause observation is auxiliary only and
must not replace BenchmarkResult.MemBytes/MemAllocs. Do not use a heap profile as
the primary byte total: runtime docs say profiles are GC-cycle snapshots whereas
ReadMemStats is current. The added Test/build can still perturb whole-binary
layout or initialization, so matched construction and old-old negative controls
remain mandatory, and diagnostic results must never be substituted into the
original qualification.

Predeclared decisions
---------------------

* First question: does old-A versus old-B reproduce repeated B/op or raw-total
  label movement at equal N? If yes, the frozen symptom is demonstrably possible
  without a source/binary difference, but V2 remains rejected under its original
  rule.
* Second: after subtracting/stratifying only by the predeclared old-old behavior,
  does old versus V2 show a repeatable difference in exact total bytes or Mallocs
  at equal N? Report effect distributions and paired raw deltas; do not invent a
  post-hoc threshold or use nonsignificance as equivalence.
* If exact Mallocs changes, add allocation-site evidence in a separately approved
  follow-up (matched alloc profiles or runtime tracing) before attributing a path.
  If MemBytes changes with identical Mallocs, inspect allocation size-class/site
  evidence. If neither exact total changes but printed q does under adaptive N,
  integer division/adaptation is directly demonstrated for this symptom.
* Independent verification must confirm hashes, source pins, command expansion,
  fixed equal N, order, sample completeness, raw arithmetic, and that no diagnostic
  result was used to alter the original V2 verdict.

Evidence required before further work
=====================================

Before any V3 implementation: a reviewed diagnostic task, completed old-old and
equal-N raw-total evidence, and a defensible candidate-specific allocation/call
site if a difference survives controls. Before any benchmark-contract revision:
separate proposal/review explaining whether the contract intends operation-local
allocations or process-wide testing.B output, plus a predeclared equivalence/noise
model validated on negative controls. Neither follows from this research alone.
